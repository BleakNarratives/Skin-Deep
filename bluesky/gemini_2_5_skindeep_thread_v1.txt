G. Yes, high-precision movement tracking from the wrist is currently available in several consumer and research-grade wearable devices. These devices typically use an Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU), which is a combination of a 3-axis gyroscope and a 3-axis accelerometer, to capture detailed kinetic metrics ``.
Consumer Smartwatches
Most modern smartwatches provide developer access to raw sensor data for tracking fine motor skills:
Apple Watch: Models like the Series 8, Ultra, and SE (2022) allow developers to pull raw 3-axis gyroscope data using the Core Motion framework . These devices can capture angular velocity at up to 4,000 degrees per second with a maximum sampling rate of 100 Hz .
Garmin: High-end models such as the Fenix 7 Pro, Approach S70, and Instinct 2 are equipped with precision gyroscopes . Their **Health SDK** allows for real-time sensor streams of accelerometer and gyroscope data .
Samsung Galaxy Watch: These devices utilize IMUs to identify specific wrist gestures and orientations in real-time ``.
Research-Grade & Specialized Wearables
For the "Gollum-style" fidelity you are looking for in the LOUGH project, there are more specialized options:
Xsens DOT: This is a lightweight, water-resistant wearable tracker specifically designed for biomechanical research. It provides high-fidelity orientation estimation and 3D kinematics by fusing data from a 3D rate gyroscope, 3D accelerometer, and 3D magnetometer ``.
EmbracePlus: Developed by Empatica, this medical-grade smartwatch provides researchers with raw, unfiltered data from a high-precision 3D accelerometer and gyroscope ``.
Mudra Band: This wearable takes it a step further by using Surface Nerve Conductance (SNC) sensors. It doesn't just measure movement; it captures neural impulses at the wrist to track the movement of each finger individually and can even sense varying fingertip pressure ``.
Key Metrics Tracked
These sensors provide a standard set of metrics necessary for performance capture:
Unbiased Rotation Rate: The rate at which the wrist rotates around three spatial axes, measured in radians per second ``.
Device Attitude: The device's absolute orientation in 3D space, typically reported as pitch, roll, and yaw or as quaternions for complex motion planning ``.
User Acceleration: The linear motion of the wrist once gravitational forces have been filtered out ``.
This sensor data can be streamed directly to platforms like the Robot Operating System (ROS) for real-time control of mechanical attachments or digital avatars ``.
