## LOGLINE

A broke operator walks into a master tattoo artist's shop with a duffel bag of
salvaged sensors and a laptop held together with grip tape — and walks out with
a handshake, a deadline, and ten thousand dollars on the line.

## SHOW BIBLE (characters / register)

- MIKE (Operator) — the engineer. Builds rigs from dumpster parts and duct
  tape. Speaks in specs and short sentences. Desperate but never pitiful.
- LANCY — master artist, Iron & Ink tattoo. Decades in the craft. Grunts
  before he agrees. Tests people without telling them they're being tested.
- SARAH (VO) — narrator. Dry, warm, mythic. Reads the data like scripture.
- THE REP — local arts council. $10K preservation grant. Polite, skeptical,
  wants "something real" by Friday.
- JET — Lancy's apprentice (introduced ep3; background presence only here).

Register: docuseries verite. Real gear, real prices, real stakes. Cold open,
escalating demo, human moment, cliffhanger. Roast interstitials optional
(DeepSeek/AI commentary on-screen text). No score until the human moment.

## BEATS (from the season arc)

1. COLD OPEN — Iron & Ink storefront. Neon flicker. Antiseptic and Marlboros.
   Mike arrives with the duffel: two webcams duct-taped to a welding helmet,
   an Arduino wired to a Vlad Blad machine, grip-taped laptop.
2. THE RIG — Lancy watches, unimpressed, as Mike sticks $50 MyoWare EMG
   sensors (salvaged from a university dumpster) to his forearm.
   "This feels like a cheap polygraph."
3. THE DEMO — SKINDEEP.html boots. Flickering neon glows on the screen like a
   Vegas slot. Lancy tattoos a simple line on practice skin. Three views:
   ARTIST POV (hand tracking + green target line), SKIN POV (cross-section,
   needle depth at the dermal junction), DATA PANEL (EMG 0.78mV spikes,
   RPM steady 7200).
4. THE MOMENT — Lancy squints. Points at the EMG waveform: "That little
   fucker there. That's when I knew the line was straight." He just
   identified his own muscle-memory signature. The system works. (No score;
   room tone; the handshake.)
5. CLIFFHANGER — Shop door opens. A client walks in — not for a tattoo.
   "I heard you're doing some tech shit." Arts council rep. $10K
   preservation grant. They want to see "something real" by Friday.
   Smash cut to black.
